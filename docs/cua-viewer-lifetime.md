# A Cua viewer launch's lifetime

`computer/cua-host/viewer-lifetime.ts` holds one Cua viewer launch's absolute lifetime: the most time, from when the launch starts, that anything opened for it may stay open. It is the lifecycle piece of a future adapter that shows Cua's own HTML5 viewer through Pim, over spacesd 0.7.0's `/mcp` and `/media`. Nothing uses it yet: it adds no route, setting or host behavior, and `eval-install` doesn't bundle it.

## Why Pim keeps its own deadline

Cua (at [`fca2193`](https://github.com/trycua/cua/tree/fca21937bc4e1420a23d544d11dbdf987f0266e6)) checks a media ticket only when `/media` upgrades (`cua-spacesd-desktop/src/grpc/media_ws.rs`, `upgrade`); `serve` then runs with no expiry timer. A media session's `attach_deadline` reaps it only while no viewer is attached (`cua-spacesd-session/src/media/session.rs`). So an attached viewer outlives its ticket, and minting a new ticket can't shorten or end anything. Pim ends the launch itself, at a deadline fixed when the launch starts. The two expiries are separate: a ticket that expires stops new upgrades upstream and doesn't end the launch; a launch that ends closes everything though its ticket is still valid.

## The contract

- **`startViewerLaunch({ lifetimeMs, clock, timers })`** starts one launch. `lifetimeMs` is whole milliseconds, 1 to `MAX_LAUNCH_MS` (4 hours); anything else throws. The deadline is the whole millisecond at or before the clock's first reading, plus `lifetimeMs`: a safe integer, so a fractional reading such as `performance.now()`'s gives up less than a millisecond and never gains any, and a reading too large for that sum to be exact throws. `clock` is monotonic (`performance.now()` by default) and `timers` sets one timer at a time (`setTimeout`, unref'd, by default); tests give their own. `timers.set` must call back later, never from within itself.
- **The deadline can't move.** The launch has `live`, `adopt`, `end`, `status` and `signal`, and is frozen; none of them takes a time. Nothing reads the browser's `expiresAt` or an upstream ticket's expiry. A reconnect adopts its transports into the same launch, so it ends at the same time; a new launch is a new grant from Pim, never a reconnect's doing.
- **Wall-clock time is converted once.** If Pim's launch record says `notAfter` in Unix milliseconds, `lifetimeUntil(notAfter, Date.now())` turns it into `lifetimeMs` when the launch starts, refusing a time that has passed or is further than `MAX_LAUNCH_MS` off. That one reading of the host's wall clock is trusted; afterwards only monotonic elapsed time counts, so turning the wall clock back extends nothing. A clock that reads earlier than before gives no time back, and one that throws or reads a non-finite time ends the launch (`clock`).
- **Authority is read on every operation, not only when the timer fires.** `live()` reads the clock and ends the launch at or after its deadline. The adapter calls it before each RPC it forwards and before it starts an upstream or media open; a timer that runs late never lets a late call through.
- **Every opened transport is handed to `adopt`, and every one reports its closure.** `adopt(kind, { close })` takes a transport (`media-viewer` and `media-upstream`, the two halves of a media relay, or `presence-rpc`) and always answers `{ admitted, closed }`. The adapter calls `closed()` when the transport reports itself closed (even if it already had), whether it was admitted or not. If the launch is not live, or already holds `MAX_LAUNCH_TRANSPORTS` (8) admitted transports not yet reported closed, the transport is refused (`admitted: false`): its `close` is called at once, and it counts in `status().refused` until its `closed()`. So an open that finishes after the deadline ends there, and is still accounted for if its close didn't take. The launch keeps admitted transports in a map of at most 8 and refused ones only as a count, so memory stays bounded. Refusing one past the bound doesn't end the launch. `signal` aborts when a shutdown is requested, for opens still under way.
- **`close` ends a network transport and nothing else,** at once, such as `ws.terminate()` or `socket.destroy()`. It must not signal a process or run a command. The launch calls it once per admitted transport when the deadline passes (`expired`), the owner calls `end()` (`ended`), the clock fails (`clock`), or the timer can't be set (`timer`), and once for each refused transport when it refuses it. One that throws is counted in `closeFailures`, stops none of the others, and leaves its transport counted as open. The signal aborts and every transport is asked to close before the timer is cleared, and a `clear` that throws is ignored, as a timer that fires after the launch ended does nothing. Later calls to `end()` change nothing, and the first reason stays.
- **Requested shutdown and observed closure are told apart.** `status().phase` is `live`, then `closing` once a shutdown is requested and while any transport handed to `adopt`, admitted or refused, hasn't reported itself closed, then `closed` once every one has. Asking a transport to close, even without an error, is not counted as its closure. `closed` means only that these network transports reported closed: it is not quiescence of the computer, and nothing here fences or stops what runs on the desktop.
- **Nothing accumulates.** A transport that reports itself closed leaves the launch, or its count. There is one timer at a time, re-set for what is left if it fires early, and cleared when a shutdown is requested.

## Tests

`computer/cua-host/test/viewer-lifetime.test.ts` (part of `pnpm --filter pim-cua-host test`) turns a clock and timers by hand and uses in-memory transports; an upstream ticket is a deadline the test keeps beside the launch. Only its last test uses Node's own clock and a 20 ms timer. They show the launch ending while a ticket is valid and staying live after one expires; a late attach refused while the timer is still pending, and the late timer doing nothing more; an early timer re-set; an asynchronous open finishing after expiry closed at once and counted until it reports closed; fifty reconnects neither lengthening the launch nor leaving registrations behind; a clock that goes back, and a wall clock turned back after conversion; refused lifetimes, wall-clock times and clock readings, including ones too large to add to exactly; ordinary fractional readings (two for which the floating-point sum isn't exact) starting launches that end no later than the start plus the lifetime; refused late and over-bound transports whose close throws, counted until they report closed, through the launch's end; a failing clock, a timer that can't be set again, and a `clear` that throws, with every transport still closed and the signal aborted; every transport closed once when cleanups throw, the launch `closing` until each reports itself closed; duplicate `end` and `closed`; an adopt from inside a cleanup refused; the transport bound; and timers cleared. No socket, server or process is involved, and how the adapter wires real `/media` and `/mcp` connections to it is not written or tested.
