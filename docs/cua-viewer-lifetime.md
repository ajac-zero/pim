# A Cua viewer launch's lifetime

`computer/cua-host/viewer-lifetime.ts` holds one Cua viewer launch's absolute lifetime: the most time, from when the launch starts, that anything opened for it may stay open. It is the lifecycle piece of a future adapter that shows Cua's own HTML5 viewer through Pim, over spacesd 0.7.0's `/mcp` and `/media`. Nothing uses it yet: it adds no route, setting or host behavior, and `eval-install` doesn't bundle it.

## Why Pim keeps its own deadline

Cua (at [`fca2193`](https://github.com/trycua/cua/tree/fca21937bc4e1420a23d544d11dbdf987f0266e6)) checks a media ticket only when `/media` upgrades (`cua-spacesd-desktop/src/grpc/media_ws.rs`, `upgrade`); `serve` then runs with no expiry timer. A media session's `attach_deadline` reaps it only while no viewer is attached (`cua-spacesd-session/src/media/session.rs`). So an attached viewer outlives its ticket, and minting a new ticket can't shorten or end anything. Pim ends the launch itself, at a deadline fixed when the launch starts. The two expiries are separate: a ticket that expires stops new upgrades upstream and doesn't end the launch; a launch that ends closes everything though its ticket is still valid.

## The contract

- **`startViewerLaunch({ lifetimeMs, clock, timers })`** starts one launch. `lifetimeMs` is whole milliseconds, 1 to `MAX_LAUNCH_MS` (4 hours); anything else throws, as does a clock reading so large that adding the lifetime would round or overflow. `clock` is monotonic (`performance.now()` by default) and `timers` sets one timer at a time (`setTimeout`, unref'd, by default); tests give their own.
- **The deadline can't move.** The launch has `live`, `adopt`, `end`, `status` and `signal`, and is frozen; none of them takes a time. Nothing reads the browser's `expiresAt` or an upstream ticket's expiry. A reconnect adopts its transports into the same launch, so it ends at the same time; a new launch is a new grant from Pim, never a reconnect's doing.
- **Wall-clock time is converted once.** If Pim's launch record says `notAfter` in Unix milliseconds, `lifetimeUntil(notAfter, Date.now())` turns it into `lifetimeMs` when the launch starts, refusing a time that has passed or is further than `MAX_LAUNCH_MS` off. That one reading of the host's wall clock is trusted; afterwards only monotonic elapsed time counts, so turning the wall clock back extends nothing. A clock that reads earlier than before gives no time back, and one that throws or reads a non-finite time ends the launch (`clock`).
- **Authority is read on every operation, not only when the timer fires.** `live()` reads the clock and ends the launch at or after its deadline. The adapter calls it before each RPC it forwards and before it starts an upstream or media open; a timer that runs late never lets a late call through.
- **Transports are adopted when they have opened.** `adopt(kind, { close })` registers a transport (`media-viewer` and `media-upstream`, the two halves of a media relay, or `presence-rpc`) and answers an attachment whose `closed()` the adapter calls when the transport reports itself closed, even if it already had. If the launch is not live, the transport is closed at once and nothing is answered, so an open that finishes after the deadline ends there. `signal` aborts when a shutdown is requested, for opens still under way. A launch holds at most `MAX_LAUNCH_TRANSPORTS` (8) transports not yet reported closed; past that, the newcomer is closed and the launch goes on.
- **`close` ends a network transport and nothing else,** at once, such as `ws.terminate()` or `socket.destroy()`. It must not signal a process or run a command. The launch calls it once per transport when the deadline passes (`expired`), the owner calls `end()` (`ended`), or the clock fails (`clock`); one that throws is counted in `closeFailures` and stops none of the others. Later calls to `end()` change nothing, and the first reason stays.
- **Requested shutdown and observed closure are told apart.** `status().phase` is `live`, then `closing` once a shutdown is requested and while any transport hasn't reported itself closed, then `closed` once every one has. `closed` means only that these network transports reported closed: it is not quiescence of the computer, and nothing here fences or stops what runs on the desktop.
- **Nothing accumulates.** A transport that reports itself closed leaves the launch. There is one timer at a time, re-set for what is left if it fires early, and cleared when a shutdown is requested.

## Tests

`computer/cua-host/test/viewer-lifetime.test.ts` (part of `pnpm --filter pim-cua-host test`) turns a clock and timers by hand and uses in-memory transports; an upstream ticket is a deadline the test keeps beside the launch. Only its last test uses Node's own clock and a 20 ms timer. They show the launch ending while a ticket is valid and staying live after one expires; a late attach refused while the timer is still pending, and the late timer doing nothing more; an early timer re-set; an asynchronous open finishing after expiry closed at once; fifty reconnects neither lengthening the launch nor leaving registrations behind; a clock that goes back, and a wall clock turned back after conversion; refused lifetimes, wall-clock times and clock readings; a failing clock; every transport closed once when cleanups throw, the launch `closing` until each reports itself closed; duplicate `end` and `closed`; an adopt from inside a cleanup refused; the transport bound; and timers cleared. No socket, server or process is involved, and how the adapter wires real `/media` and `/mcp` connections to it is not written or tested.
